Lesson 5: Checking normality

Many tests (t-tests, ANOVA, correlation) assume your numeric data follows a bell-shaped curve, called a normal distribution. Before using them, you should check.

What you will learn

  • Why normality matters
  • How to run the Explore command
  • How to read the Shapiro-Wilk test
  • How to read a histogram and Q-Q plot

Step 1: Run Explore

  1. Click Analyze → Descriptive Statistics → Explore.
  2. Move Score into Dependent List.
  3. Click Plots, tick Normality plots with tests and Histogram, then click Continue.
  4. Click OK.

Step 2: Read the Tests of Normality table

Example output with made-up numbers.

The rule

  • Sig. greater than .05: the data is not significantly different from normal. You can assume normality.
  • Sig. less than .05: the data is not normal. Consider a non-parametric test.
Tip: Use Shapiro-Wilk for small samples (under about 50). It is the more reliable one.

Step 3: Look at the pictures

  • Histogram: roughly a bell shape, centred, with no big skew to one side.
  • Normal Q-Q plot: the dots should follow the diagonal line closely.
  • Skewness: in the Descriptives table, values between -1 and +1 are usually fine.

What if my data is not normal?

Parametric test Non-parametric alternative
Independent samples t-testMann-Whitney U
Paired samples t-testWilcoxon signed-rank
One-way ANOVAKruskal-Wallis
Pearson correlationSpearman correlation
Tip: With very small samples, normality tests have little power. Always look at the charts too, not only the p-value.
