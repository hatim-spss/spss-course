Lesson 4: Charts and graphs

Charts help you see your data before you test it. Each chart type suits a different kind of variable.

What you will learn

  • Which chart to use for which variable
  • How to make a histogram
  • How to make a bar chart
  • How to make a scatterplot
  • How to edit and save a chart

Which chart should I use?

Your data Best chart
One numeric variable (Age, Score)Histogram
One categorical variable (Gender, Faculty)Bar chart
Two numeric variablesScatterplot
Numeric variable across groupsBoxplot

Chart 1: Histogram

  1. Click Graphs → Chart Builder. If a warning appears, click OK.
  2. In the bottom list, choose Histogram.
  3. Drag the first picture into the preview area.
  4. Drag Score onto the x-axis, then click OK.

A histogram shows how values are spread. Look for the shape: is it centred, or pushed to one side?

Chart 2: Bar chart

  1. Click Graphs → Chart Builder.
  2. Choose Bar and drag the first picture into the preview.
  3. Drag your categorical variable (for example Score_Group from Lesson 3) onto the x-axis.
  4. Click OK.
Tip: If the bars show an average instead of counts, change the y-axis statistic to Count in Element Properties.

Chart 3: Scatterplot

  1. Click Graphs → Chart Builder.
  2. Choose Scatter/Dot and drag the first picture into the preview.
  3. Drag Age to the x-axis and Score to the y-axis.
  4. Click OK.

Each dot is one student. If the dots rise from left to right, the two variables move together.

Edit and save your chart

  1. Double-click the chart in the Output window to open the Chart Editor.
  2. Double-click titles, labels or bars to change them.
  3. Close the editor, then right-click the chart and choose Copy to paste it into Word.
Tip: Give every chart a clear title and labelled axes. Examiners notice.

Practice

Make a histogram of Score and a scatterplot of Age against Score. What do you notice?
